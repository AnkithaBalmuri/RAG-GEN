BIG BANG THEORY

Introduction

The Big Bang Theory is the leading scientific explanation for the origin and evolution of the universe. It states that the universe began approximately 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot and dense state.

Development of the Theory

Georges Lemaître proposed that the universe expanded from a "primeval atom." Edwin Hubble later discovered that galaxies are moving away from one another, supporting cosmic expansion.

Stages of the Big Bang

1. Initial expansion
2. Formation of fundamental particles
3. Formation of atoms
4. Formation of stars and galaxies
5. Evolution into the present universe

Evidence

Cosmic Microwave Background

A faint radiation discovered in 1965 that represents leftover heat from the early universe.

Expansion of the Universe

Galaxies are moving away from each other.

Abundance of Light Elements

Observed hydrogen and helium levels match Big Bang predictions.

Importance

The theory explains:

* Cosmic origins
* Galaxy formation
* Element formation
* Large-scale structure

Current Research

Scientists continue investigating:

* Inflation
* Dark matter
* Dark energy
* Conditions before the Big Bang

Interesting Facts

* The universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old.
* Space itself has been expanding since the Big Bang.
* The theory does not describe what existed before the earliest moments.

Summary

The Big Bang Theory provides the foundation of modern cosmology and explains the evolution of the observable universe.
