SOLAR SYSTEM

Introduction

The Solar System consists of the Sun and all objects gravitationally bound to it, including planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets. It formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.

The Sun

The Sun contains over 99 percent of the Solar System's mass and provides light and energy to all planets.

Planets

The eight planets are:

Inner Planets

* Mercury
* Venus
* Earth
* Mars

Outer Planets

* Jupiter
* Saturn
* Uranus
* Neptune

Dwarf Planets

Examples include:

* Pluto
* Eris
* Haumea
* Makemake
* Ceres

Small Bodies

The Solar System contains:

* Asteroids
* Comets
* Meteoroids
* Kuiper Belt Objects

Formation

The Solar System formed through the Solar Nebula Theory, where gas and dust collapsed under gravity and gradually formed planets.

Exploration

Major missions include:

* Voyager
* Cassini
* Juno
* New Horizons
* Mars rovers

Importance

The Solar System serves as a natural laboratory for studying planetary science, astronomy, and the origins of life.

Interesting Facts

* Jupiter is the largest planet.
* Venus is the hottest planet.
* Neptune has the strongest winds.
* Earth is the only known planet with life.

Summary

The Solar System is a complex planetary system centered on the Sun and remains one of the primary focuses of space exploration and astronomical research.
