Introduction
Global Positioning System (GPS) satellites form a space-based navigation network that allows users to determine precise location, velocity, and time anywhere on Earth.

History
GPS was originally developed by the United States Department of Defense for military purposes before becoming available for civilian use.

How GPS Works
The system consists of:

Space Segment

Control Segment

User Segment

At least 24 satellites continuously orbit Earth.

Principles of Navigation
GPS receivers calculate location by measuring signals from multiple satellites.

The process uses:

Trilateration

Atomic clocks

Radio signals

Applications
GPS is used in:

Smartphones

Aviation

Shipping

Agriculture

Surveying

Emergency services

Military operations

Scientific Importance
GPS supports:

Earth science

Climate monitoring

Disaster management

Geophysical studies

Future Developments
Modern GPS systems provide greater accuracy and reliability through upgraded satellites and signals.

Summary
GPS satellites have transformed navigation and become essential infrastructure for modern society.
