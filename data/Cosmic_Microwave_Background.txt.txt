Introduction
The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) is the oldest observable light in the universe. It represents radiation left over from the Big Bang and provides a snapshot of the universe when it was only 380,000 years old.

The CMB is one of the strongest pieces of evidence supporting the Big Bang Theory.

Discovery
In 1965:

Arno Penzias

Robert Wilson

discovered a faint microwave signal coming from all directions.

This signal was identified as relic radiation from the early universe.

Origin
After the Big Bang:

The universe was hot and dense.

Light could not travel freely.

About 380,000 years later:

Atoms formed.

Radiation escaped.

The CMB was created.

Properties
Temperature
Average temperature:

2.725 Kelvin

Uniformity
Very uniform across the sky.

Tiny Fluctuations
Small temperature differences reveal early matter distributions.

Importance
The CMB helps scientists determine:

Age of the universe

Composition of the universe

Expansion rate

Early cosmic structure

Major Missions
COBE
First detailed measurements.

WMAP
Improved precision.

Planck Mission
Produced the most detailed CMB map.

Scientific Significance
CMB observations support:

Big Bang Theory

Cosmic inflation

Dark matter studies

Dark energy research

Summary
The Cosmic Microwave Background is relic radiation from the early universe and provides one of the most important tools for understanding cosmology.
