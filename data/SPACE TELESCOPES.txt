SPACE TELESCOPES
Introduction
Space telescopes are astronomical observatories that operate outside Earth's atmosphere. Unlike ground-based telescopes, space telescopes can observe the universe without interference from atmospheric turbulence, weather conditions, air pollution, and atmospheric absorption of electromagnetic radiation.

Since their introduction in the twentieth century, space telescopes have transformed astronomy and provided unprecedented views of planets, stars, galaxies, nebulae, black holes, and the early universe. They have enabled scientists to answer fundamental questions about the origin, evolution, and structure of the cosmos.

Space telescopes are among the most important scientific instruments ever developed and continue to play a critical role in modern astronomical research.

History of Space Telescopes
The concept of placing telescopes in space was first proposed by astronomers during the early twentieth century.

Scientists recognized that Earth's atmosphere limits observations by:

Distorting incoming light

Absorbing certain wavelengths

Creating weather-related interruptions

In 1946, astronomer Lyman Spitzer published a paper advocating for large space telescopes.

His vision eventually led to the development of modern orbital observatories.

Major milestones include:

Orbiting Astronomical Observatory (OAO)
Launched during the 1960s.

Provided valuable ultraviolet observations.

Hubble Space Telescope
Launched in 1990.

Revolutionized astronomy with high-resolution observations.

Chandra X-ray Observatory
Launched in 1999.

Designed for X-ray astronomy.

Spitzer Space Telescope
Launched in 2003.

Focused on infrared observations.

James Webb Space Telescope
Launched in 2021.

The most advanced space telescope ever built.

Why Space Telescopes Are Necessary
Earth's atmosphere affects astronomical observations.

Problems include:

Atmospheric Turbulence
Causes stars to appear to twinkle.

Reduces image quality.

Weather Conditions
Clouds block observations.

Rain and storms limit observing time.

Atmospheric Absorption
Many wavelengths never reach the ground.

Examples include:

X-rays

Gamma rays

Most ultraviolet radiation

Much infrared radiation

Space telescopes eliminate these limitations.

Advantages of Space Telescopes
Clear Images
No atmospheric distortion.

Continuous Observation
Can operate day and night.

Access to Entire Spectrum
Observe wavelengths blocked by Earth's atmosphere.

Improved Sensitivity
Detect extremely faint objects.

Long-Term Monitoring
Observe objects continuously for months or years.

These advantages make space telescopes essential for modern astronomy.

Exoplanet Discoveries
Space telescopes have transformed the search for planets beyond the Solar System.

Methods include:

Transit Method
Measures brightness decreases when planets pass in front of stars.

Direct Imaging
Captures images of exoplanets.

Spectroscopy

Thousands of exoplanets have been discovered using space telescopes.

Summary
Space telescopes are among humanity's greatest scientific achievements. By operating beyond Earth's atmosphere, they provide clear, detailed observations of the universe across the electromagnetic spectrum. From discovering distant galaxies and exoplanets to studying black holes and the early universe, space telescopes continue to revolutionize our understanding of the cosmos and inspire future generations of scientists and explorers.
