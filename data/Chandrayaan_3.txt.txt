Introduction
Chandrayaan-3 is India's third lunar exploration mission developed by ISRO. Its primary objective was to demonstrate safe lunar landing technology and rover operations on the Moon.

Background
After Chandrayaan-2's lander lost communication during descent, ISRO carefully analyzed the mission and improved landing systems for Chandrayaan-3.

Mission Components
The mission consisted of:

Propulsion Module

Vikram Lander

Pragyan Rover

Unlike Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3 did not include an orbiter because the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter remained operational.

Launch
Launch Vehicle: LVM3-M4

Launch Date: July 14, 2023

Launch Site: Satish Dhawan Space Centre

Historic Landing
On August 23, 2023, the Vikram lander successfully touched down near the Moon's south polar region.

India became:

Fourth country to soft-land on the Moon

First country to land near the lunar south pole

Pragyan Rover
The rover carried instruments to analyze:

Lunar soil composition

Mineral content

Surface characteristics

The rover traveled across the lunar surface conducting scientific experiments.

Scientific Objectives
Demonstrate safe landing technology

Study lunar geology

Analyze elemental composition

Investigate south polar environment

Global Significance
The mission attracted worldwide attention because the lunar south pole is considered important for future human exploration due to possible water ice deposits.

Summary
Chandrayaan-3 was a historic success that significantly strengthened India's position in global space exploration.
