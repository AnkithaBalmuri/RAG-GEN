Introduction
SETI stands for Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence. It is the scientific effort to detect evidence of intelligent civilizations beyond Earth.

Scientists search for signals, technologies, or other signs that may indicate the existence of advanced extraterrestrial life.

History of SETI
Project Ozma
Conducted in 1960 by astronomer Frank Drake.

First modern SETI experiment.

Development
Radio telescopes became the primary tools for SETI research.

Today, searches continue worldwide.

Drake Equation
Developed by Frank Drake.

Estimates the number of communicative civilizations in the Milky Way.

Factors include:

Star formation rates

Planet occurrence

Life development

Technological civilizations

Search Methods
Radio Signals
Most common approach.

Scientists monitor stars for unusual transmissions.

Optical Signals
Search for laser pulses.

Technosignatures
Evidence of advanced technology.

Examples:

Artificial radio signals

Megastructures

Industrial pollution

Fermi Paradox
Question:

"If intelligent life is common, where is everybody?"

Possible explanations include:

Rare life

Rare intelligence

Civilizations self-destruct

Signals are difficult to detect

Major Facilities
Allen Telescope Array
Dedicated SETI observatory.

Green Bank Telescope
Used for signal searches.

FAST Telescope
World's largest radio telescope.

Current Research
Scientists investigate:

Technosignatures

Exoplanet habitability

Artificial signals

Future detection methods

Scientific Importance
SETI helps answer one of humanity's greatest questions:

"Are we alone in the universe?"
