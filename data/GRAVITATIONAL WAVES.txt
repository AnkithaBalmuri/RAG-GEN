GRAVITATIONAL WAVES
Introduction
Gravitational waves are ripples in the fabric of spacetime caused by the acceleration of massive objects. Predicted by Albert Einstein in 1916 as part of his General Theory of Relativity, gravitational waves travel at the speed of light and carry information about some of the most violent events in the universe. Their discovery opened an entirely new branch of astronomy known as gravitational-wave astronomy.

Unlike light, gravitational waves pass almost unaffected through matter, allowing scientists to observe cosmic events that may be invisible to traditional telescopes. The detection of gravitational waves has revolutionized our understanding of black holes, neutron stars, and the evolution of the universe.

History of Discovery
Einstein's Prediction
In 1916, Albert Einstein predicted that accelerating massive objects should create distortions in spacetime that propagate outward as waves.

For decades, these waves remained undetected because they are extremely weak by the time they reach Earth.

Joseph Weber
In the 1960s, physicist Joseph Weber attempted to detect gravitational waves using large aluminum cylinders known as Weber bars.

Although his results could not be confirmed, his work inspired future research.

LIGO Detection
On September 14, 2015, the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) made the first confirmed detection of gravitational waves.

The signal originated from two merging black holes approximately 1.3 billion light-years away.

This historic discovery was announced in February 2016.

What Are Gravitational Waves?
Gravitational waves are disturbances in spacetime generated when massive objects accelerate.

Examples include:

Black hole mergers

Neutron star collisions

Supernova explosions

Orbiting binary stars

As these waves travel through space, they slightly stretch and compress spacetime.

How Gravitational Waves Form
According to General Relativity, mass and energy curve spacetime.

When massive objects move rapidly:

Spacetime becomes disturbed.

Ripples propagate outward.

Energy is carried away.

Waves travel across the universe.

The stronger the acceleration and mass, the stronger the gravitational waves produced.

Properties of Gravitational Waves
Travel Speed
They travel at the speed of light.

Extremely Weak
By the time they reach Earth, the distortions are incredibly small.

Carry Energy
Gravitational waves transport energy away from their sources.

Pass Through Matter
Unlike light, they can travel through gas, dust, and stars with minimal interaction.

Sources of Gravitational Waves
Black Hole Mergers
The most commonly detected sources.

When two black holes spiral together and merge, enormous gravitational waves are produced.

Neutron Star Collisions
Generate both gravitational waves and electromagnetic radiation.

Supernovae
Exploding stars may create detectable gravitational waves.

Binary Star Systems
Orbiting compact stars continuously emit weak gravitational waves.

Detection Methods
LIGO
Located in the United States.

Uses laser interferometry to measure tiny spacetime distortions.

Virgo
Located in Italy.

Works alongside LIGO.

KAGRA
Located in Japan.

Uses underground facilities to reduce noise.
