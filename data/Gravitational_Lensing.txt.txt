Introduction
Gravitational lensing occurs when a massive object bends the path of light due to gravity. This phenomenon was predicted by Einstein's General Theory of Relativity and has become one of the most powerful tools in modern astronomy.

How It Works
Massive objects curve spacetime.

Light follows these curved paths.

As a result:

Images become distorted.

Objects appear magnified.

Multiple images may form.

Types of Gravitational Lensing
Strong Lensing
Produces:

Einstein Rings

Multiple images

Large distortions

Weak Lensing
Creates subtle distortions.

Used to map dark matter.

Microlensing
Occurs when smaller objects bend light.

Useful for exoplanet discovery.

Einstein Ring
When alignment is perfect:

Light forms a circular ring.

Called an Einstein Ring.

These are rare and scientifically valuable.

Applications
Dark Matter Mapping
Shows invisible matter distribution.

Galaxy Studies
Magnifies distant galaxies.

Exoplanet Detection
Used in microlensing surveys.

Scientific Importance
Helps scientists study:

Dark matter

Galaxy clusters

Cosmology

Early universe objects
