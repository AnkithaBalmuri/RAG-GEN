Introduction
Mangalyaan, officially known as the Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), was India's first interplanetary mission and the first mission to Mars developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation. The mission demonstrated India's capability to design, launch, navigate, and operate a spacecraft in deep space. Mangalyaan became one of the most successful and cost-effective Mars missions ever conducted.

History and Development
The idea for a Mars mission emerged after the success of Chandrayaan-1. ISRO scientists wanted to demonstrate India's ability to perform complex interplanetary missions.

The project was approved in 2012 and developed within a very short timeframe of approximately 15 months.

The mission's estimated budget was about ₹450 crore, making it one of the least expensive Mars missions in history.

Launch and Journey
Launch Date: November 5, 2013

Launch Vehicle: PSLV-C25

Launch Site: Satish Dhawan Space Centre

Because PSLV could not directly send the spacecraft to Mars, the spacecraft performed multiple Earth-orbit raising maneuvers before entering a Mars transfer trajectory.

After traveling nearly 680 million kilometers through space, the spacecraft successfully entered Mars orbit on September 24, 2014.

Scientific Instruments
Mars Color Camera

Methane Sensor for Mars

Thermal Infrared Imaging Spectrometer

Lyman Alpha Photometer

Mars Exospheric Neutral Composition Analyzer

Major Achievements
First Asian nation to reach Mars orbit

First country to reach Mars on its maiden attempt

Demonstrated deep-space communication

Demonstrated interplanetary navigation

Operated far longer than its planned six-month mission

Scientific Contributions
The mission studied:

Martian atmosphere

Dust storms

Surface features

Seasonal changes

Atmospheric escape processes

Importance
Mangalyaan established India as a major spacefaring nation and inspired future deep-space missions.

Summary
Mangalyaan remains one of ISRO's greatest achievements and a landmark in global planetary exploration.
